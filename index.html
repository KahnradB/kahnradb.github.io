About

I'm a doctoral candidate in mathematics at Johns Hopkins University, where I study the geometry of surfaces that evolve under mean curvature flow. I also hold an M.S. in Systems Engineering, and I apply the same analytical habits to reliability, risk, and simulation problems in industry and government research.

My thesis work concerns self-shrinkers, the surfaces that shrink homothetically under mean curvature flow and model its singularities. Alongside that, I build statistical and simulation tools: Bayesian reliability analysis at the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, multi-simulation studies at Sedaro, and earthquake and hurricane hazard models for infrastructure resilience research.

I care a great deal about teaching. I've taught more than 500 students across eight courses at Hopkins and have twice received the department's Joel Dean Award for Excellence in Teaching.

Research

Preprint · Submitted 2026

A stable self-shrinking Möbius bundle in ℝ⁴

K. Braxton, T. K. Lee, J. Zhu

arXiv:2609.19648

Self-shrinkers are the blow-up limits of mean curvature flow, so understanding which ones are stable tells us which singularities a generic flow can actually produce. My research studies a codimension-two self-shrinking Möbius band in four-dimensional space and computes its Morse index and nullity by decomposing the stability operator into Fourier modes and analyzing each sector.

The work combines Sturm–Liouville and Maslov index techniques with high-precision numerical certification, so that every spectral claim is backed by both proof and computation.
